Installing

  1. Go to the latest release and download the file called Thicket-Setup-version.exe.
  2. Run it, and follow the steps.

Thicket installs for your Windows account only, so it doesn't need an administrator password. It's added to the Start menu; a desktop icon is optional. It needs 64-bit Windows 10 or 11.

"Windows protected your PC"

Windows shows this blue box the first time you run a downloaded program that isn't signed with a paid certificate. Thicket isn't signed (certificates cost hundreds of pounds a year), so you'll see it once.

Click More info, then Run anyway.

Every release lists the checksum of each file (SHA256SUMS.txt), if you'd like to check your download is the real one.

The first start

Thicket asks where you record:

  • United Kingdom: the UK species list is built in, so there's nothing to download.
  • A US state: that state's species pack downloads in a few seconds and is checked before it's installed.

Then add your first sighting with the Add sighting button, or press Ctrl+N.

Upgrading

Download the newer installer and run it. It replaces the program and keeps everything else: sightings, photos, settings and backups. If Thicket is open, the installer asks you to close it first.

Thicket used to be called WildLife. Installing Thicket over WildLife upgrades it the same way.

Where your data lives

In the folder %LOCALAPPDATA%\WildLife on your PC. (It keeps the app's first name, so nothing went missing in the rename.) To open it, press Win+R, paste that name and press Enter. Thicket also shows the full path in Settings › Data.

Installing, upgrading and uninstalling never touch this folder.

Backups

Thicket backs up by itself once a day and when you close it, and checks each backup can be read back. It keeps the last 7 days, 4 weeks and 12 months.

Do one thing: choose a backup folder on another drive, such as a USB drive or a second disk. Until you do, backups sit next to your data, so they wouldn't survive that drive failing. Go to Settings › Backup › Backup folder. Back up now is there too.

Restoring a backup

Go to Settings › Backup › Restore… and pick a backup. Your data goes back to how it was then. Your current data is kept as a safety copy first, and Thicket restarts to finish.

If Thicket ever can't open your data when it starts, it offers to restore the latest backup.

Deleted something by mistake? Deleted sightings and photos wait in the recycle bin for 30 days (Settings › Data), and most changes can be undone straight away.

Importing from iNaturalist

  1. On the iNaturalist website, use Export observations to download your observations as a CSV file.
  2. In Thicket, go to Settings › Import & export › From iNaturalist and choose the file.
  3. Check what Thicket found, and tick Also download the photos if you'd like them.

Importing a newer export later only adds what's new or changed. Spreadsheets import the same way, from From a spreadsheet.

Changing region

Go to Settings › Region. You can switch between the UK and US states, or add another state's pack for a trip. Packs only add species: your sightings never change.

Uninstalling

Open Windows Settings › Apps › Installed apps, find Thicket and choose Uninstall. Your data is kept, and the uninstaller tells you where. To remove it as well, delete the %LOCALAPPDATA%\WildLife folder afterwards (and your backup folder, if you chose one).

Something's wrong

  • Settings › Data › Health check checks your data file, every photo, your backups and the species list, and explains anything it finds.
  • Still stuck? Report it on GitHub. Say what you did and what happened. Settings › About › Open log folder has Thicket's log files, which help. Have a look at them before attaching one: they can include folder and place names.
